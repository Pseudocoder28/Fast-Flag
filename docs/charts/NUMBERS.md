## Latency from crash onset: race control vs us

Source: latency_by_type.csv / .md / .png. Onset = speed below 50% of the car's own speed at that point on its previous 3 clean laps, for 1 s (full rule in latency_by_type.md). Latency on crashes with a clear collapse, which is what our detectors are best at: not a recall figure. ## Escalation scorecard: our VSC, SC and red recommendations vs race control

Source: escalation.json, escalation.md and escalation.png (python -m src.eval.escalation). In-sample: the detector settings were tuned and the race control rules set on these races. Counterfactual: assumes race control acted on our recommendation at once.

- 51 official escalations in 20 training races (VSC 23, SC 25, red 3). We recommended a VSC, SC or red for 29 (56.9%), 27 of them earlier than the race control feed; median lead 20.0 s (middle half 9.1 to 33.9 s).
- Missed 22: 7 with a car collapse we could see, 15 without one (re-flags of an earlier stoppage, debris, weather, lap 1).
- Escalations race control never made: 24 in 31.2 race hours = 0.77 per race hour (20 where race control kept yellows only, 4 with no official flag).
- Flag choice: same first flag as race control in 22 of 29; our 59 recommendations by flag: VSC 17, SC 34, red 8. FastF1 puts stopped cars on the racing line, so the lateral offset cannot tell VSC from SC; an impact can: "SC after an impact, VSC otherwise" separates race control's VSCs from its SCs and reds correctly 18 of 26 times, "always SC" 13.
