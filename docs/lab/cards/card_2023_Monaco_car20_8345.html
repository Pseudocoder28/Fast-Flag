WHAT AM I LOOKING AT?

MAG (car 20) suddenly lost speed on lap 58: from about 71 km/h to 0 km/h. We call that moment the start of the crash, and every time on this card is counted from it: it is 0 on the timeline and on both charts.

Our system raised its first alert 2.2 seconds after the crash began, because the car had stopped where cars normally drive fast. Race control's first yellow flag came 2.8 seconds after the crash began. So our alert was 0.5 seconds earlier than the race control feed.

We recommended a Virtual Safety Car 12.2 seconds after the crash began. Race control handled this crash with yellow flags only, with no Virtual Safety Car, Safety Car or red flag.

Why the car count matters: every car that passes a crash at full speed goes past wreckage, debris and marshals before its driver has been told to slow down. In the first 60 seconds after this crash, 2 cars did that, about one every 30 seconds. Every second a flag waits adds to that number.

WORDS USED ON THIS CARD

Yellow flag
danger ahead: slow down, no overtaking.
Double yellow flag
serious danger: slow down a lot and be ready to stop.
Virtual Safety Car (VSC)
every driver must slow to a set, lower speed, everywhere on the track.
Safety Car (SC)
a road car leads the whole field slowly so marshals can clear the track safely.
Red flag
the race is stopped.
Race control
the officials who decide the flags. Their messages are the official feed we compare against.
Marshal sector
one of about 20 numbered stretches of the track, each watched by marshals (trackside safety staff).
Racing speed
at least 80% of that car's usual speed at that exact spot, so the driver has not slowed down.

Honest limits: this is a replay of historical race data, not a live race. The counts show how the cars actually drove. We don't claim every driver would have slowed down sooner, only how many were exposed while the flag was not out. We compare against the race control feed: marshals at the trackside may already have been waving flags.
